# Evolvable structure: patterns for people

Part of `../principles.md`. Patterns 3, 5, 10, 11 and 12: defaults, personal content, what people see, ownership by adapting, one method across cases. Each ideal states its aim, then its evidence. Source pointers read `file > heading`, with files in `../sources/`. Quotes are verbatim, and Joshua's typed inputs keep their typos.

## Patterns

3. **Defaults people can change.** A sensible default lets it work with no setup; the
   person override it, and let them pin a choice so it stops changing under them. Choose the default
   by reversibility: reversible things proceed, forks and irreversible acts hold. A default that acts
   for every user is best paired with a setting each user can change, and a stored default is kept
   distinct from the person's own choice (a stored flag records whether the person chose). New
   behaviour ships default-off, and new settings default to the old behaviour.

5. **A shared container with personal content.** The rhythm, artifacts and review structure are the
   same for everyone, and the individual part is defined (the goal, the metric, the method, the framing)
   separately for each person. His teaching statement treats "each student's experience as a separate
   research project including metrics and preliminary experiments to see what works", with the same
   three evaluation criteria for every student. The same applies to a course, a protocol, a plan and
   a UI.

10. **People see only what is set, and move between high and low level.** Settings nobody has
    touched stay hidden, the rest are grouped, and a graph of parts can collapse into one part and
    open again, so the surface does not grow with the number of options.

11. **People own it by adapting it.** The structure is editable: a person can copy it, change it
    and keep it, and still be told when the original improves. Ownership comes from adapting, not
    from receiving. "Nonetheless, teachers and students are free to edit or adapt the propositions
    and development map however they wish. Adaptation is how a clear personalization process enables
    autonomy." A template passes a new version on to its copies, except the fields each person has
    already changed.

12. **One method, with categories emerging from each case.** The same method serves cultures, people
    and sources, and the features come out of the data instead of one fixed instrument forced on
    every case: "Consequently, unbiased theoretical analysis requires contextual
    factors to emerge from the data." A fixed universal scale fails some cases (the Greyson scale in his
    near-death-experience research). In the dissertation, labels are kept out of the ranking.
