## Purpose: progress over time and across iterations

**The point of evolvable structure is progress over time.** `SKILL.md` puts the weight on the
farthest point in the future we can reasonably plan for: fast progress now may be the best route
there, but that is a judgment to make, not an assumption. A structure is adaptable when the next
iteration is cheaper and better than the last one: you can try an alternative without a rewrite,
compare it against what came before, keep what won, and carry what you learned into the next case.
Generality that does not make the next iteration easier is not adaptability, it is overhead. Every
principle below serves this one. (Stated by Joshua, 2026-10-14: "the adaptable structure enables
progress over time and iterations, that is the purpose of the adaptable structure"; and in
`SKILL.md`.)

To size the structure for a task, ask what the far purpose needs: which parts will likely need to
change (keep those changeable, as parameters, definitions or content) and which are more useful
stable (keep those in the core). That choice is the structure's niche.

What this looks like in practice (more, in his frameworks, under "Examples" at the end: success as
continual improvement, a frozen-dataset experiment workflow, a refined source-of-truth document,
settled battles kept as a dated list, a funnel lesson carried forward):
- His Project Documentation board names "Comparing alternative solutions for iterative improvement" as
  one of its two focuses, separates a definition from its implementations so "the defined solution can
  be carried out in different ways which continue to improve, and it should be clear what "improving"
  means", and ends its steps with "You're done! Or, keep improving, adjusting any problems, goals, and
  solutions as necessary."
- Lessons from one case are applied to the rest: after the generic landing page improved, "Improve the
  other pages based on what we learned/types of changes we mnade t ogeneric one ." (2026-05-22).
- What a session learns has to reach the next one: "Captured feedback or context must be
  automatically applied the next time the same situation happens, or it's worthless."
- A lost experiment is kept with its numbers so it is not re-proposed, and a wrong method stays as a
  comparison arm: "keep a wrong method as a comparison arm to quantify the gain."
- Iteration is gated on evidence: "Success must be demonstrated at each stage before more spend".

**A process is a structure too.** The same idea applies to how people work together: the stable part
is how ideas are proposed, checked and decided, and what it lets change is the work, and in the end
the process itself. A good one holds the rule for changing itself, stages commitment so cheap and
reversible steps come before costly ones, keeps a steady rhythm so people can plan, gives silence and
rejection a defined outcome so ideas do not die by neglect, and handles bias and power through its
design rather than through trust in one person.

When you structure anything, ask what the next iterations will likely change and make those changes
parameters, definitions or content rather than edits to the core. Record what each iteration learned
where the next one will find it (saved parameters, versions, notes, the skill files).
